# Church OS
### One machine, in your building, that runs the back office so you can do the ministry.

> **Status, stated plainly.** Church OS is a proposal, not a product you can
> buy today. The public app and its intake forms are built. The giving,
> attendance, membership, and pastoral-care modules are specified and unbuilt.
> No machine has been purchased and no pilot has begun. This document is the
> request to start one.

---

## The problem

A church runs on records. Who is a member, who has not been seen in a month, who
gave what and when, who volunteered, what is on the calendar, who needs a call.

For most churches that lives in a dozen places: a spreadsheet, a giving app, a
mailing list, a filing cabinet, and the pastor's memory. Each one is a monthly
bill, a separate login, and a copy of the congregation's most private
information sitting on some company's server far from the building.

Pastors did not answer a call to do data entry. Staff burn hours on admin that
should go to people.

## The offer

**Church OS will be one machine that lives in your building and runs the back
office.**

It will keep every record in one place. It will draft the newsletter, reconcile
the giving, track attendance, run the calendar and the text blasts, and quietly
tell the pastor "these twelve families have not been here in a month" so he can
reach out. It will answer questions in plain language: *"How did giving compare
to last year?"* *"Who volunteered for the food drive?"* *"Draft a note to the
new visitors from Sunday."*

It assists the pastor and the staff. It does not replace them, and it never
touches the spiritual heart of the church. It gives you your week back.

## The promise that matters most

**Your records live in your building.**

Church OS will run on your own machine, on your own premises, owned by you. Your
members' records, your giving history, your pastoral-care notes: they live on
your box, they are not sold, and they are not mined. In a moment when
congregations rightly distrust Big Tech with their people, this is the whole
point.

**The one thing that leaves the building is an encrypted backup**, held as
ciphertext that we cannot read, because a church that loses its records to a
fire or a failed drive has lost something irreplaceable. The key stays with the
church.

Money is always in human hands. The system tracks, reconciles, and drafts the
report, but a person approves every transaction and everything is logged for the
audit. Pastoral notes are held under the tightest access controls in the
system, and until the machine is in the building they stay exactly where they
are today.

## Why Shiloh first

Shiloh Baptist Church and the Faith Community Development Corporation are the
**proposed** proving ground. If they say yes, we run the real operation on it
for a season and measure what it saves in hours and in families re-engaged, and
Shiloh becomes the model every other church can see working before they say yes.

No pilot has begun. This document is the request to start one.

## The model

1. Prove it at Shiloh.
2. Package it as one standard machine with setup and support.
3. Install it into churches across the Faith CDC network and beyond.

Every install is a church that owns its own operation instead of renting it from
five different companies. It is the FREEDOM mission in a box: build capacity,
keep it local, put the tools in the community's own hands.

## The ask

We are looking for [the blessing to run the Shiloh pilot / the funding to build
the flagship / a partner to help us reach the first ten churches].

The machine is roughly $3,500 in hardware, plus battery backup and a backup
drive. The exact comparison against what the organization spends today on
scattered software will be priced once we have inventoried those subscriptions,
which is the first thing the discovery interview does. After the first install,
the recurring cost is support rather than licenses.
